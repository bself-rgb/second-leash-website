When to refer

Pet-related barriers can show up in many human-service settings.

If a client has a temporary crisis and no safe plan for their dog, Second Leash may be able to help coordinate a solution.

🏠

Housing & homelessness

Shelters, housing programs, rapid rehousing, housing navigators, and case managers.

💜

Domestic violence

Survivors who need a safe temporary plan for a companion animal while relocating or entering shelter.

🔄

Recovery & treatment

People entering residential treatment, recovery programs, or another temporary level of care.

⚖️

Reentry & incarceration

People preparing for incarceration, returning home, or working toward stable housing after release.

🏥

Hospitalization & health crises

Temporary situations where a client cannot safely provide routine care for their dog.

🎖️

Veterans & community services

Veteran-serving organizations and other community programs encountering pet-related barriers.

What Second Leash can coordinate

We handle the pet side so your team can focus on the person.

01

Temporary foster placement

We look for an appropriate temporary placement based on the dog's needs, expected care period, location, and available foster capacity.

02

BYOF support

If the client already has a trusted person willing to foster, Second Leash may be able to coordinate and support that arrangement.

03

Veterinary coordination

When resources permit, we can help coordinate necessary veterinary care and communicate care needs with the foster.

04

Transportation

We may help coordinate transportation to foster placement, veterinary appointments, or reunification.

05

Food & basic supplies

Available resources may allow us to help with food, medications, or other basic needs during temporary care.

06

Reunification planning

We work toward a safe, responsible return to the owner when the temporary crisis has been resolved.

The referral process

Simple for your team. Clear for the client.

1

Identify the barrier

Your team determines that a client's dog needs a temporary care plan.

2

Submit a referral

Provide the basic information we need to understand the client, dog, timing, location, and requested assistance.

3

Second Leash reviews

We assess safety, capacity, timing, transportation, veterinary needs, and potential placement options.

4

Care is coordinated

When we can accept the case, we coordinate the appropriate temporary-care plan and communication.

5

Reunification

When appropriate and safe, the dog returns to the owner according to the agreed reunification plan.

Important: Submitting a referral does not guarantee placement. Second Leash is not a 24-hour emergency shelter and does not replace emergency veterinary, animal-control, or domestic-violence crisis services.

Partnerships

Different organizations can work with Second Leash in different ways.

A referral relationship can be the starting point. As collaboration grows, organizations may explore formal MOUs, service agreements, sponsorships, grant partnerships, or other appropriate arrangements.

Referral Partner

Agrees to refer appropriate clients to Second Leash.

Community Partner

Collaborates with Second Leash around shared clients, resources, or community needs.

Resource Partner

Provides veterinary, transportation, supplies, professional services, or other support.

Funding Partner

Provides financial support, sponsorship, or an appropriate funded service arrangement.
