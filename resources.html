Arkansas Pet Resources & Temporary Dog Care

Practical resources for Arkansas pet owners, foster families, referral partners, and communities facing a temporary crisis.

Looking for help with your dog?

If you are trying to keep your dog safe and cared for while you go through treatment, hospitalization, incarceration, housing instability, domestic violence, or another temporary crisis, you are not alone. This page brings together pet-care and human-service resources that may help you keep your dog with you or find safe temporary care.

Need temporary foster care through Second Leash? We provide temporary foster care for dogs when their owners are temporarily unable to care for them. Start a temporary-care request or learn how our program works.

Prepare a Temporary-Care Packet

If someone else will care for your dog temporarily, having complete information ready can make the transition safer and easier.

  • Proof of ownership or authority to arrange care
  • Veterinary records and vaccine history
  • Medication names, doses, and instructions
  • Feeding routine, food brand, allergies, and dietary restrictions
  • Behavior history, triggers, bite history, and escape risks
  • Microchip information and registration details
  • Emergency contacts and authorized decision-makers
  • Expected care period and communication plan

For Referral Partners & Professionals

Treatment & Recovery Programs

When a client entering treatment has a dog, early planning can reduce the risk of surrender. Contact Second Leash as soon as possible so timing, consent, transportation, safety, and care needs can be assessed.

Hospitals & Social Services

Pet ownership can complicate hospitalization or crisis intervention. Referrals are most helpful when the expected timeframe and dog's basic needs are known.

Animal Welfare Partners

Emergency & Urgent Situations

Immediate danger: Call 911.

Veterinary emergency: Contact a licensed veterinarian or emergency veterinary clinic. Second Leash cannot replace urgent medical care.

Domestic violence: Use a safe device when possible and protect confidential location information. The National Domestic Violence Hotline and Safe Havens for Pets can help identify safety-planning and pet-care options.

Resource availability changes: Services, eligibility requirements, hours, geographic coverage, and funding can change. Always confirm current details directly with the organization before relying on a resource.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find temporary care for my dog in Arkansas?

Second Leash provides temporary foster care for dogs whose owners are temporarily unable to care for them because of circumstances such as treatment, hospitalization, incarceration, housing instability, domestic violence, or another crisis. Start a request here.

What if I cannot afford veterinary care?

Start with Pet Help Finder, which can help you locate financially friendly veterinary services and other pet resources by ZIP code. Your veterinarian may also know about local assistance options.

What if I am homeless and have a dog?

Arkansas 211 can connect you with local human-service resources, while Pet Help Finder and Safe Havens for Pets can help locate pet-specific assistance.

What if I am leaving domestic violence and have a pet?

Include your pet in your safety plan when it is safe to do so. The National Domestic Violence Hotline can provide safety-planning support, and Safe Havens for Pets provides a searchable directory of pet safe-haven programs.
